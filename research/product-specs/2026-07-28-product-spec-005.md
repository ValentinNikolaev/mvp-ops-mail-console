## Executive Summary

Agency Incident Workbench

## Pros & Benefits

- Solves a concrete, recurring agency/MSP task: convert scattered delivery evidence into a provider-scoped incident brief, a safe next action, and a redacted escalation packet.
- Uses an explicit evidence model rather than a deliverability score, keeping authentication, reputation, placement, provider verdicts, list/cadence, and measurement distinct.
- Confidence gates and visible missing or contradictory evidence reduce harmful “fixes” such as blind warm-up, resend, or DNS changes.
- A TypeScript modular monolith and managed infrastructure keep the first release inexpensive while preserving clean boundaries for later connectors.
- Case history, rechecks, and reusable support packets create a credible retention loop for multi-client operators.

## Cons & Risks

- Raw headers, NDRs, exports, screenshots, and support replies will be incomplete or sensitive; the product must validate inputs, redact by default, isolate tenants, and expire raw files.
- Provider filtering is partly opaque, so the system must clearly distinguish observed facts, inference, unknowns, and recommendations; it cannot guarantee inbox placement.
- The first workflow depends on manual collection. It must produce a useful brief before connectors exist, or users will abandon intake.
- A broad dashboard, portfolio analytics, and many ESP connectors would dilute the narrow validation loop and create premature integration debt.
- Rule calibration can drift; outcome feedback needs review and auditability rather than automatic confidence increases.

## Proposed Tech Stack & Tools

- **Frontend — Next.js, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, and shadcn/ui:** a fast, accessible guided case workflow, evidence board, hypothesis panel, and printable packet in one deployable application.
- **Application API — Next.js Route Handlers with Zod:** typed, server-side validation for case metadata, artifact manifests, permissions, and deterministic rule inputs without a separate service fleet.
- **Database — PostgreSQL with Prisma:** tenant, case, evidence, hypothesis, packet, audit, and recheck relations; JSONB preserves provider-specific parsed fields while the schema evolves.
- **Object storage — Cloudflare R2 or Amazon S3:** encrypted tenant-scoped raw artifacts with presigned upload/download, lifecycle deletion, and a database record of redacted derivatives.
- **Async jobs — Inngest or Trigger.dev:** retryable header/NDR/CSV parsing, normalization, packet rendering, connector polling, and scheduled rechecks separated from interactive requests.
- **Rules engine — TypeScript rule modules plus versioned fixtures:** deterministic, testable hypotheses that expose supporting, missing, and disconfirming evidence instead of opaque model scores.
- **Authentication — Clerk Organizations or Supabase Auth:** fast agency/client workspace setup, invitations, organization roles, and MFA-ready access controls.
- **Hosting — Vercel with managed PostgreSQL and R2/S3:** low idle cost, preview deployments, simple operations, and a clear path to worker and database scaling.
- **Observability — Sentry, structured JSON logs, and Postgres audit events:** trace parsing and rule failures, retain decision provenance, and replay support incidents safely.
- **Delivery — GitHub Actions and GitHub Releases:** test schema/rules on every change and publish versioned MVP artifacts after `main` is pushed.

### MVP Architecture

- Browser workbench: guided incident intake asks who is affected, where mail landed or failed, what changed, and what evidence can be provided.
- Intake API: validates tenant membership, stores case metadata and artifact manifests, and creates short-lived upload URLs; raw email content never goes into application logs.
- Normalization worker: extracts redacted facts such as SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment, sender and return-path domains, SMTP/NDR codes, provider, message timing, ESP metrics, seed observations, and operator statements.
- Evidence store: Postgres stores normalized facts with provenance, freshness, owner, sensitivity, confidence contribution, and contradiction links; object storage holds raw inputs under retention policy.
- Quality gate: checks the evidence threshold for Gmail/Outlook rejection, bounce, Spam/Junk, Promotions, silent-drop, and not-observable flows; it either permits ranked hypotheses or gives a focused collection checklist.
- Rule evaluator: produces a bounded set of provider-scoped hypotheses, supporting and missing evidence, contradictions, confidence, action risk, safe remediation, and a verification/recheck step.
- Packet service: renders a redacted client, ESP-support, or provider-escalation brief with facts, inference boundaries, and requested next action.
- Recheck scheduler: records verification outcomes and prompts follow-up without treating correlation as causal proof.

### Product Scope

- First buyer: agencies and MSPs managing repeated client email-delivery incidents.
- First incident template: mail suddenly fails at Gmail or Outlook, moves to Spam/Promotions, or begins bouncing/rejecting after a DNS, sender, content, list, cadence, ESP, or pool change.
- In scope: manual artifact intake, evidence quality gate, deterministic hypotheses, confidence-gated remediation, redacted packet export, and scheduled rechecks.
- Explicitly out of scope: sending, warm-up automation, inbox guarantees, a universal reputation score, deep provider integrations, and broad portfolio analytics.

### MVP Feature Set

- Incident intake for client/domain, identity, ESP/pool, flow, volume and cadence change, affected provider, business impact, headers/NDRs, CSV/manual metrics, seed observations, screenshots, and support replies.
- Evidence board that labels observed fact, source, freshness, sensitivity, missing evidence, contradiction, and not-observable state.
- Minimum-evidence rules and an actionable evidence-request checklist when ranking is unsafe.
- Deterministic hypotheses for auth-pass delivery failures, provider-specific filtering, shared ESP/IP reputation, content/list/cadence changes, measurement-only anomalies, forwarding/path issues, and unknown.
- Recommendation panel with confidence, action risk, prohibited unsafe actions, verification method, owner, and recheck date.
- Redaction preview, immutable audit events, and support-packet export.
- A simple case list and outcome recheck loop; no portfolio dashboard until design partners demonstrate the retention need.

### Fast, Cheap, Maintainable, Scalable Rationale

- One TypeScript application avoids service-management overhead while the evidence schema and buyer workflow are still under validation.
- Manual inputs defer expensive, brittle provider integrations and let design partners identify the connector that genuinely earns retention.
- Postgres plus JSONB supports both stable case relationships and variable provider fields without prematurely modeling every mail system.
- Jobs isolate parsing and rendering failures, are independently retried, and can move to separate workers as volume grows.
- Versioned deterministic rules are inspectable, fixture-testable, and safer than opaque automated advice for a reputation-sensitive workflow.
- Tenant-scoped storage, redaction, audits, and retention are cheaper to build into the first data path than to retrofit after customers upload mail artifacts.

### Operational Tradeoffs

- The agency/MSP wedge favors repeat incidents and packet reuse over a single-ESP product experience.
- Refusing to rank low-evidence cases makes the product less magical, but preserves trust and prevents harmful remediation.
- Manual-first onboarding creates friction, so the first output must be immediately shareable and materially better than a spreadsheet.
- Connector work remains behind an `EvidenceSource` contract; only the first paid-retention connector should be implemented after design-partner validation.

### Open Questions

- What exact evidence threshold should permit a ranked hypothesis for Gmail/Outlook placement, rejection, bounce, and not-observable incidents?
- Which ten to twenty real agency/MSP incidents can test the evidence schema and compare recommended versus known outcomes?
- Which packet type earns adoption first: client-facing brief, ESP support ticket, provider escalation, or internal remediation memo?
- Which pricing unit best reflects value: client workspace, monitored domain, active incident, packet export, or seat?
