## Executive Summary

Evidence Packet Workbench

## Pros & Benefits

- Converts scattered sensitive artifacts into a consistent provider-scoped case and support packet without promising placement outcomes.
- The canonical evidence model, deterministic rules, and explicit uncertainty make guidance auditable, testable, and safe to evolve.
- A modular TypeScript monolith keeps build and support cost low while preserving boundaries for parsers, rules, storage, and connectors.
- Agency/MSP workspaces create repeat use, historical benchmarks, and a reusable client-reporting retention loop.

## Cons & Risks

- Provider filtering is opaque and input evidence incomplete; unsafe inference must be impossible to present as fact.
- Headers, NDRs, exports, and screenshots require tenant isolation, redaction, encrypted storage, retention limits, and immutable audit trails.
- Manual intake can become friction; v1 must demonstrate faster case preparation before investing in connectors.
- A packet alone may not earn recurring spend; validate a measurable improvement with design partners.

## Proposed Tech Stack & Tools

- **Next.js + TypeScript + Tailwind + shadcn/ui:** one accessible guided case workbench with fast iteration and no separate frontend fleet.
- **Next.js Route Handlers + Zod:** typed request validation, authorization checks, and a small API surface for cases, evidence manifests, packet generation, and rechecks.
- **PostgreSQL + Prisma:** normalized tenant/case/evidence/rule/audit relations; JSONB retains provider-specific parsed fields without premature schema sprawl.
- **Cloudflare R2 or Amazon S3:** encrypted tenant-scoped raw artifacts, presigned transfer, lifecycle deletion, and redacted derivatives; never log raw email content.
- **Inngest or Trigger.dev:** retryable asynchronous parsing, redaction, packet rendering, and scheduled rechecks outside request latency.
- **Versioned TypeScript rule modules + fixtures:** 8–12 deterministic rules returning confirmed/suspected/insufficient evidence, supporting facts, alternatives, safe action, and verification.
- **Clerk Organizations or Supabase Auth:** agency/client workspaces, invited roles, MFA-ready controls, and tenant-scoped authorization.
- **Vercel + managed PostgreSQL + R2/S3:** inexpensive previews and low operations for the modular-monolith stage; workers can split later.
- **Sentry + structured JSON logs + Postgres audit events:** observability without sensitive raw artifacts, rule replay, and supportable operational history.

### MVP Architecture

- Browser case workspace accepts four evidence forms: headers, NDRs, ESP delivery/bounce rows, and seed observations.
- Intake service validates membership and metadata, writes an immutable artifact manifest, and issues short-lived storage URLs.
- Normalization worker extracts identity, auth alignment, route, recipient provider, SMTP/NDR codes, pool/IP, time, and sensitivity into the canonical evidence schema.
- Evidence graph stores fact provenance, freshness, contradictions, confidence contribution, and retention state; raw source remains separate in encrypted object storage.
- Completeness gate decides whether a bounded rule evaluation is safe or returns the smallest evidence-request checklist.
- Rule evaluator groups evidence by provider/pool, produces transparent hypotheses and reversible runbook tests, then feeds a redaction-aware packet renderer.
- Packet/recheck service exports a client, ESP, or provider brief and records outcome without attributing causal success absent evidence.

### Product Scope

- First buyer: agencies/MSPs handling Gmail and Microsoft 365 client delivery incidents.
- First outcome: a defensible redacted escalation packet and decision record produced faster than the existing spreadsheet/ticket process.
- In scope: manual read-only intake, evidence normalization, completeness check, deterministic rules, packet export, recheck, audit, and retention controls.
- Out of scope: sending, mailbox write access, automatic remediation, warm-up, universal reputation scoring, continuous monitoring, collaboration suite, and broad connectors.

### MVP Feature Set

- Guided incident intake for client/domain, sender/ESP/pool, flow, volume/cadence changes, provider, impact, evidence, and message IDs.
- Evidence board and timeline labelling observed fact, inference, source, freshness, sensitivity, contradiction, and not-observable state.
- Rule cards with rule version, confidence, alternatives, missing evidence, prohibited unsafe actions, safe next test, owner, and recheck date.
- Provider/ESP support-packet template, redaction preview, immutable audit events, and evidence expiry.
- Pilot metrics: time-to-triage, time-to-escalation, packet acceptance, recurrence, and user-rated actionability.

### Fast, Cheap, Maintainable, Scalable Rationale

- One TypeScript codebase and managed services minimize operational overhead while validating product-market fit.
- Manual, read-only artifacts avoid brittle OAuth and connector support until a retained-value case exists.
- Postgres relations plus JSONB support strict evidence provenance alongside variable provider fields.
- Background jobs isolate parsing/rendering failures; they can move to dedicated workers without redesigning the product.
- Rule fixtures make recommendations testable against historical cases and safer than opaque automated advice.

### Operational Tradeoffs

- Rejecting insufficient-evidence cases may feel less magical but preserves trust and prevents costly unsafe action.
- Agency/MSP focus prioritizes repeat workflows over a polished single-sender experience.
- Redaction/retention controls add early work but prevent data-governance debt from uploaded mail artifacts.

### Open Questions

- Which 20–30 cases and buyer partners will establish a credible time-to-triage baseline?
- What packet type earns paid adoption first: provider escalation, ESP support, client report, or internal handoff?
- Which connector should follow the pilot, based on measured retention rather than perceived integration completeness?
